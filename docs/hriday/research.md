## Documentation discrepancies investigated

The general v6 setup page currently emphasizes client tokens; the migration guide and official union type explicitly permit client ID. The specific architecture/H2 client-ID requirement is supported. No new token endpoint is needed for the documented one-time checkout.

The API reference and raw OpenAPI describe some dispute requests too generically to generate a complete adapter. The official integration guide shows the multipart evidence structure and a JSON accept-claim request. Use those examples to design the spike, then treat observed sanitized request/response pairs as the integration fixtures. Do not fill gaps by inventing fields.

Some original `docs.paypal.ai` links redirect to inaccessible `developer.paypal.com/docsai` locations. The old Transaction Search guide returns 404. The current official links above provide the relevant facts. Failed fetches were not counted as verification.

## Prior art and pitch claims

Two primary vendor sources support the source documents' differentiation, without proving novelty:

- [Chargeflow's PayPal material](https://www.chargeflow.io/category/paypal-disputes-chargebacks) advertises dispute automation and tracking synchronization. Fairfold must demonstrate the packing-to-buyer-report loop and batch prevention; “AI answers disputes” alone does not distinguish it.
- [Razorpay's badge description](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-trusted-business-badge-eligibility/) describes a checkout trust badge based on business checks and transaction/service history. It is comparison research only; Fairfold adds no Razorpay integration.

No exhaustive market search, merchant interview, willingness-to-pay study, conversion uplift, avoided-dispute measurement or worldwide novelty has been established in this pass. Do not repeat the internal document's count of competing hackathon repos as a current verified count. Do not state that PayPal has no overlapping product anywhere. Keep the source-defined audience and experience; support differentiation with the workflow actually shown.

The $38 hoodie is a cross-border USD demo. The ₹799 versus ₹1,160 example is an illustrative comparison from the idea document, not the fee charged on that USD order. The safe factual demo claim is that dispute costs can be material for small exporters; any numeric fee displayed needs its currency and applicable conditions.

The source document's legal analysis is a design rationale, not a compliance certification. This pass did not re-audit DPDP, GDPR, FCRA or product-safety law. Keep the specified data boundaries, rights, neutral wording and human controls, and avoid claiming legal approval.
