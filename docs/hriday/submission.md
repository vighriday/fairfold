## Claims and labels

- Label all PayPal activity **Sandbox**, courier scans **Simulated**, and cross-merchant network signals **Simulated**. Fictional stores and synthetic buyers must not be confused with real reputation data.
- The B17 population/history is seeded; the checkout/capture/dispute response is a real sandbox API flow. Show which is which. Do not call the seeded 18-order hold a measured production outcome.
- A verified hash chain supports integrity of the recorded sequence. It does not prove a parcel's contents on arrival or guarantee a dispute outcome.
- Every review theme links to its underlying verified review records and includes merchant replies. No uncited AI adjective or accusation about a real store.
- Use the actual measured photo/eval counts supplied by Ishaan. Leave absent results marked not run; remove unsupported accomplishments from public text.
- Do not describe Fairfold's Store Report as inserted inside PayPal's own checkout popup. The hackathon implementation places it beside the button on the merchant page; native PayPal placement is the production pitch only.
- Display real vendor debug/response identifiers only where useful; hide app secrets, bearer tokens, buyer email/street addresses and account passwords. Use fictional labels and authorized assets; no copyrighted music.

The [India fee page](https://www.paypal.com/in/business/paypal-business-fees) supports the exporter market and fee table, but the ₹1,160 example is not the charge on the live $38 USD transaction. Keep that distinction visible or use a nonnumeric cost statement. The [Seller Protection policy](https://www.paypal.com/in/legalhub/paypal/seller-protection) explains the not-as-described coverage gap; Fairfold does not change that policy.
